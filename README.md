## 1. Project Overview

This project applies supervised machine learning to detect and localise thermal degradation in a laboratory steam cracker furnace coil. A high-temperature camera monitors 18 spatial zones across 6 heating elements at 1-minute resolution over 28 repeated cracking–decoking cycles spanning 6 weeks.

The core approach is to learn the relationship between heater power input (setpoint %) and coil temperature during early, nominally healthy cycles, then use deviations from that learned relationship — residuals — as a degradation signal in later cycles. Degradation is tracked both at the furnace-wide level and localised to individual elements and zones, enabling identification of which specific coil sections are approaching operational temperature limits.

**Key questions addressed:**
- Can a regression model trained on early cycles predict coil temperatures in later cycles, and how does prediction error evolve?
- Does steady-state temperature, zone-to-zone spread, or thermal equilibration time drift systematically across cycles in a manner consistent with progressive coking?
- Can a classifier reliably distinguish nominal from degraded operating conditions without ground-truth coking annotations?

The 66 columns are: `Timestamp`, `Setpoint %`, `Emissivity`, 18 zones × 3 statistics (Min, Avg, Max), `TI 144` × 3, `TI 145` × 3, `BGCOMP` × 3.

### Furnace Layout

The furnace contains **6 heating elements** arranged vertically (Element 1 at top, Element 6 at bottom). Each element is monitored across **3 spatial zones** (left, centre, right), giving 18 zones total:

| Element | Zones | Columns |
|---|---|---|
| Element 1 (Top) | 0, 1, 2 | Zone 0 Min/Avg/Max … Zone 2 Min/Avg/Max |
| Element 2 | 3, 4, 5 | Zone 3 … Zone 5 |
| Element 3 | 6, 7, 8 | Zone 6 … Zone 8 |
| Element 4 | 9, 10, 11 | Zone 9 … Zone 11 |
| Element 5 | 12, 13, 14 | Zone 12 … Zone 14 |
| Element 6 (Bottom) | 15, 16, 17 | Zone 15 … Zone 17 |

`TI-144` and `TI-145` are refractory (furnace box ambient) thermocouples — they measure the bulk thermal environment surrounding the coil, not the coil itself.

### Experimental Protocol

```
~12 h  Ramp-up   →  SP rises from 0 % to ~100 %
~11 h  Cracking  →  SP held at ~100 % (cracking temperature conditions)
~1 h   Ramp-down →  SP falls from ~100 % to ~36 %
~11 h  Decoking  →  SP held at ~36 % (decoking/burn-off conditions)
        [repeat next day]

Mon–Fri: 5 cycles per week
Sat–Sun: furnace shutdown
6 weeks total → 30 planned cycles → 28 usable cycles (1 anomaly excluded)
```

## 6. Data Preparation — Assumptions and Design Decisions

This section documents every non-trivial decision made in `data_prep.py` and the reasoning behind it.

### 6.1 Sentinel Value: 799 °C

**What it is:** 799 °C is the lower detection limit of the high-temperature cameras. The cameras report this value when the furnace temperature falls below the measurable range — during cold startup, weekend shutdown, or ramp-down to near-ambient.

**What we do:** Every occurrence of 799 is replaced with `NaN` across all temperature columns (zone Min/Avg/Max, BGCOMP, TI-144, TI-145). Rows are retained but flagged via `all_zones_valid = False`.

**Why not drop these rows:** Cold-period timestamps define cycle boundaries and shutdown periods. Dropping them would create time gaps that complicate regime labelling.

---

### 6.2 Operating Regime Labels

Every row is assigned one of six labels based on setpoint value and direction of change:

| Label | Criterion | Physical meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `cracking_ss` | SP ≥ 99.9 % | Steady-state cracking hold |
| `decoking_ss` | 35.5 % ≤ SP ≤ 36.5 % | Steady-state decoking hold |
| `ramp_up` | SP rising, between decoking and cracking bands | Heating toward cracking temperature |
| `ramp_down` | SP falling, between cracking and decoking bands | Cooling toward decoking temperature |
| `shutdown` | SP = 0 % | Weekend shutdown |
| `other` | SP > 0 % but below decoking band | End-of-decoking artifact; not used in modelling |

**Why 99.9 % for cracking (not 100 %):** The PID controller holds at ~99.9–100.0 % with minor fluctuations. A hard 100.0 % threshold would exclude valid steady-state rows. 99.9 % cleanly captures the full hold without admitting ramp rows.

**Why 35.5–36.5 % for decoking:** The nominal decoking setpoint is 36 %. Inspection confirms the controller holds within ±0.5 % of this target, so the ±0.5 % band reliably isolates the hold.

**Ramp direction:** Determined from the sign of `Setpoint %.diff()`. Positive → ramp-up; negative → ramp-down.

---

### 6.3 Cycle Identification

Cracking and decoking cycles are identified as contiguous blocks of their respective steady-state labels. Blocks with fewer than 300 rows are discarded as startup artifacts — these are ~33-row fragments caused by brief low-level setpoint activity at the start of each week before the main ramp begins.

The 28 decoking blocks align 1-to-1 with the 28 usable cracking blocks by calendar position.

**Cycle numbering:** Raw cracking blocks are numbered 1–29 chronologically. After excluding the anomalous block (raw Cycle 7), the remaining 28 are re-indexed 1–28. Decoking cycles are numbered 1–28 independently.

---

### 6.4 Anomalous Cycle: Raw Cycle 7 (December 9–11)

**What happened:** The cracking block starting December 9 ran continuously for ~48 hours (2,871 rows) with no decoking phase in between. All other normal cracking blocks are 619–657 rows (~10–11 hours).

**Why excluded:** This block violates the standard protocol and represents a structurally different operating condition. Including it would corrupt both the regression training set and the cycle-over-cycle degradation trend analysis.

**Detection:** Any cracking block with more than 1,000 rows is flagged as anomalous. The block is never assigned a `cycle_id` (retains `cycle_id = 0`). All SS output datasets filter to `cycle_id > 0`. A timestamp-based sanity check confirms no rows from this period appear in the outputs.

---

### 6.5 Equilibration Time

**Definition:** Minutes from the first row of a steady-state phase until the furnace is considered thermally stable.

**Criterion:** The rolling standard deviation of `furnace_avg` (mean of all 18 zone averages) over a 10-minute window first drops below 5 °C. A minimum window size of 10 rows is enforced — the std is only evaluated once a full 10-minute window is populated, preventing spuriously small values in the first few rows.

**Why this matters:** Equilibration time is a potentially informative health indicator. Progressive coke deposition increases thermal resistance in the coil walls, which may alter how quickly — or to what temperature — the furnace equilibrates after reaching setpoint. This is tracked alongside the steady-state temperature level rather than cropped away.

**Observed values:** Cracking cycles equilibrate in 9–18 minutes. Monday cycles (after the 2-day cold shutdown) consistently take longer (13–18 min) than mid-week cycles (9–11 min), which is physically expected and confirms the criterion is behaving correctly.

---

### 6.6 TI-144 and TI-145 Covariate Lag

**Why included:** Refractory temperature is a physically meaningful covariate — a hotter ambient furnace box causes coil temperatures to be higher at the same power setpoint.

**Why lagged:** The refractory temperature is partly a response variable, driven by the element temperatures it surrounds. Using contemporaneous TI values as inputs to predict element temperatures would introduce circular leakage. A **5-minute lag** is applied, using the ambient temperature as a leading indicator rather than a simultaneous readout.

**Implementation:** Lag is computed on the full chronologically sorted dataframe before any phase subsetting, ensuring the lag spans true calendar time rather than resetting at phase boundaries.

---

### 6.7 Train / Test Split

**Method:** Chronological. Cycles 1–17 (~60 %) form the training set; Cycles 18–28 (~40 %) form the test set.

**Why not random:** Random splitting causes data leakage — test rows from mid-experiment would be temporally adjacent to training rows from the same cycle, giving the model implicit access to the test period's operating conditions. Chronological splitting simulates real deployment: a model trained on early healthy cycles monitors later cycles where degradation accumulates.

## 8. Modelling — Design Decisions

### Feature Engineering

Features are built per-cycle so that lag and rolling computations never cross cycle
boundaries (the furnace state resets between cycles). The feature set for each target:

- **Setpoint %** at the current timestep
- **Lagged setpoint** at [1, 5, 10, 30] minutes — captures thermal inertia at multiple timescales. Lag-2 was dropped (near-identical to lag-1, adds collinearity without signal).
- **Lagged element averages** at [1, 5, 10, 30] minutes — six elements × four lags = 24 features encoding the recent thermal state of the furnace
- **Rolling 30-minute mean and std of `furnace_avg`** — smoothed thermal state signal; 30 minutes chosen to capture slow background drift without excessive noise
- **TI-144 and TI-145 (5-minute lag)** — refractory temperatures already lagged in `data_prep.py`

The equilibration transient is cropped from the start of each cycle before features are
computed. This ensures models learn only from thermally stable steady-state data.

### Regression

One model per target × per phase = 24 models total (12 targets × 2 phases).
Three model types are compared: OLS (baseline), Ridge, and Lasso.

**Shared alpha selection:** Rather than running cross-validation independently per
target, a single alpha is selected for Ridge and Lasso via 3-fold CV on
`furnace_roll_mean` as a representative target. All 12 targets use this shared alpha.
This is justified because all targets share the same feature matrix — the optimal
regularisation strength is a property of the feature space, not the individual target.

**Alpha grid:** [0.01, 0.1, 1, 10]. CV consistently selected alpha=0.01 for both
Ridge and Lasso across both phases, indicating only mild regularisation is needed.

**Best model per target:**
- Element average targets (6): OLS wins in cracking (7/12 overall), confirming the
  SP→temperature relationship is well-conditioned and regularisation adds nothing
- Max-zone targets (6): Lasso dominates, indicating sparse feature selection is
  beneficial for predicting localised hot spots
- Notably, Elem3_maxzone is the weakest target (cracking R²=0.944, RMSE=2.3°C),
  likely due to complex thermal interactions at the mid-furnace position

### Residual Analysis

Residuals are computed post-equilibration only (equilibration rows are already cropped
in feature engineering). Per-cycle statistics computed for each target:
- **Mean residual** — systematic model bias; positive drift indicates the coil is
  running hotter than predicted, consistent with progressive coke deposition
- **Max absolute residual** — largest single-timestep deviation within a cycle
- **CUSUM final value** — cumulative sum of residuals; a consistently positive CUSUM
  indicates systematic underprediction building across the cycle

### Degradation Classification

**Labelling:** A timestep is labelled degraded if its 30-minute rolling mean residual
exceeds the baseline mean + 2σ for any of the 12 targets (union rule). The baseline
distribution is estimated from the first 12 training cycles.

The 2σ threshold and 12-cycle baseline were arrived at iteratively:
- Run 1 (1σ, 8 cycles): 76.9% degraded rate — near-trivial classifier
- Run 2 (2σ, 12 cycles): 43.6% degraded rate — balanced and physically meaningful

See `analysis_notes.md` for the full iteration history and rationale.

**Classifier:** Leave-one-cycle-out (LOCO) logistic regression with balanced class
weights. Features are rolling mean and std residuals for all 12 targets plus setpoint.
LOCO is used rather than standard k-fold CV because the data has temporal structure
and cycles must be treated as the unit of holdout to avoid leakage.
